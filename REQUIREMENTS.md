# AfterImpact — Requirements

AfterImpact helps a person manage the aftermath of a car accident: paperwork, accident-related health issues, and getting life back on track.

This document owns WHAT the system must do. Security and privacy requirements are authored in `SECURITY.md`; the visual and interaction language is `DESIGN.md`; components, technology choices, and data flow are `ARCHITECTURE.md`. Requirements elaborate the recorded decisions in **Decisions**; `TO BE DECIDED` marks an unresolved value inside an otherwise clear requirement.

## Scope

In scope (v1): everything in **Functional Requirements** below — single-user accounts, accident cases, document storage, health tracking, tasks/deadlines/reminders, contacts, expenses, journal/timeline, and export.

Out of scope (v1):

- Multi-user access of any kind: sharing, collaboration, helper/attorney accounts (D-2, D-11).
- Integrations with insurers, healthcare systems, or any other third-party data source or service (D-7; the two external service dependencies D-7 permits — email delivery and the managed identity service — are infrastructure, not integrations).
- Automated claim filing or form generation for specific insurers/agencies.
- Medical or legal advice of any kind (FR-4.9, FR-5.8).
- Payments or monetization features.
- Languages other than English and Spanish (NFR-5.4); currencies other than one per-case currency (FR-7.5) (D-15).
- SMS and push notification channels (D-13).

## Data Entities

Logical model only; storage design belongs to `ARCHITECTURE.md`.

- **User** — email (unique), registered passkey credentials, notification preferences, timezone (IANA identifier, detected and submitted by the client at registration and changeable in settings — FR-1.12), locale (seeded the same way and changeable in settings — FR-1.12; English when unset, NFR-5.4), last successful sign-in timestamp (the only clock FR-9.5 inactivity runs on).
- **Device** — an opaque random identifier for one browser or app installation, with first-seen and last-seen timestamps, belonging to one User; SEC-AUTHN-11 compares against it to decide whether a sign-in comes from a recognized context. An account MUST hold at most 10 devices: recording an eleventh MUST evict the least recently seen. Devices are purged with the account (FR-9.3) and by the inactivity purge (FR-9.5), are included in the account export (FR-9.2), and are disclosed in the privacy policy (SEC-DATA-7).
- **Case** — title, accident date (required), accident time/location/description (optional), currency (one per case, set at creation — FR-7.5), status (open, archived), owned by one User. All entities below belong to exactly one Case (except User and Device).
- **Document** — original file, title, category, optional document date, notes, tags, links to Contact/Health Issue/Expense.
- **Health Issue** — name, optional body area, onset date, severity (0–10), status (active, improving, resolved), description; has Progress Updates and Treatments.
- **Progress Update** — timestamp, note, optional severity (0–10).
- **Treatment** — name, start/end dates, prescribing provider (Contact), notes.
- **Appointment** — date/time, provider (Contact, optional), location, purpose, linked Health Issues, outcome notes.
- **Task** — title, optional due date, category, notes, done flag + completion timestamp, hard-deadline flag, linked records.
- **Reminder** — target record (Task or Appointment), fire datetime, delivery state.
- **Contact** — name, role (insurance adjuster, insurance company, attorney, medical provider, repair shop, other driver, witness, employer, other), phone/email/address, reference numbers (e.g., policy #, claim #), notes.
- **Expense** — amount, date, category, payment status, optional payee (Contact), linked receipt Document, linked Health Issue, reimbursement records.
- **Journal Entry** — user-authored text, entry date (backdatable), created/edited timestamps, optional linked Contact/records.
- **Timeline Event** — system-generated record of a case action (see FR-8.1).
- **Export Job** — requested scope, status, produced artifact.
- **Notification** — an in-app feed item (type, target record, created timestamp, read state) surfaced to the user. `type` is a closed set of exactly four values — reminder (FR-5.6), security message (SEC-AUTHN-11), export ready (FR-9.1, FR-9.2), and delivery failure (SEC-EXT-4) — validated like every other enumeration (SEC-INPUT-1, D-47). An item becomes read only when the user opens it or its target record, and a mark-all-read action MUST exist; merely viewing the feed MUST NOT mark anything read, so a security message is never silently consumed by a glance (D-47, T-27). A notification tied to a case or record is removed with it (FR-2.6) and with the account (FR-9.3), and notifications are included in the account export (FR-9.2). Modeling the feed as a data entity lets retention, purge, and export rules attach to items that may carry health-adjacent text (T-43).

**Sensitivity classification.** Every entity above is sensitive personal data under D-5. Separately, and for on-screen protection only, sensitivity is classified at entity level: Health Issue, Progress Update, Treatment, Appointment, and Journal Entry records are flagged sensitive in full, as are a Document's title and notes; Case, Task, Contact, and Expense metadata is not flagged. `DESIGN.md` **On-Screen Privacy** masks the flagged set and SEC-SESSION-7's keyboard-flag rule binds the same set; both consume this classification rather than restating it (D-31).

**Field length limits.** Every free-text field carries an enforceable bound, validated server-side by SEC-INPUT-1 and refused with the plain-language message NFR-5.3 requires (D-32):

- Titles and names — Case title, Document title, Health Issue name, Treatment name, Task title, Contact name — MUST be at most 200 characters.
- Notes, descriptions, and outcome notes — Case description, Document notes, Health Issue description, Progress Update note, Treatment notes, Appointment outcome notes, Task notes, Contact notes, Expense notes — MUST be at most 10,000 characters.
- A Journal Entry's text MUST be at most 50,000 characters.
- A Document tag MUST be at most 50 characters, and a Document MUST carry at most 25 tags.
- A Contact reference number MUST be at most 100 characters.
- Short free text — Case and Appointment location, Appointment purpose, Health Issue body area, and Contact address — MUST be at most 500 characters; a Contact phone number and a Contact email address MUST each be at most 200 (D-41).

## Functional Requirements

### 1. Accounts & Authentication

The credential ceremonies, session mechanics, and enumeration-resistance controls that implement this section are specified in `SECURITY.md` **Authentication** and **Session management**.

- **FR-1.1** The system MUST allow a person to register with an email address and MUST send a verification email; the account MUST NOT be usable for sign-in until the email address is verified.
- **FR-1.2** The system MUST use passkeys (WebAuthn) for normal account access and MUST NOT offer passwords or any user-chosen shared-secret credential (D-9). The system MUST also accept a one-time code sent to the submitted address to verify it and bootstrap the first passkey, or to the verified account address to recover from loss of every passkey; an email-code ceremony MUST NOT grant access to case or account data.
- **FR-1.3** The system MUST sign in a user to a normal account session only after a valid passkey ceremony on a verified account. A valid email-code ceremony MUST create only the restricted bootstrap/recovery session FR-1.2 defines. Every failed passkey or email-code ceremony MUST show the same generic error (SEC-AUTHN-5 owns what a failure may disclose).
- **FR-1.4** After 5 consecutive failed authentication attempts for an account within 15 minutes, the system MUST block further attempts for that account for at least 15 minutes, and MUST log the event (SEC-LOG-1). The block applies to failed or invalid attempts and MUST NOT prevent a successful valid passkey ceremony by the owner — a valid WebAuthn assertion is cryptographic proof of possession, not a guess (T-1). Because an account-scoped block is otherwise a denial-of-service lever anyone who knows the email can renew, source-based and anti-automation throttling applies ahead of the account block (SEC-AUTHN-4; `SECURITY.md` owns the limit values).
- **FR-1.5** The system MUST let a signed-in user sign out, immediately invalidating that session.
- **FR-1.6** The system MUST let a user who has lost access to every registered passkey recover by completing a one-time-code ceremony through the verified email address and registering a replacement passkey in the resulting restricted recovery session (D-9). The code MUST expire after 10 minutes. The recovery session MUST permit no other account or case operation; `SECURITY.md` specifies the rest of the flow (SEC-AUTHN-6).
- **FR-1.7** The system MUST let a signed-in user change their email address, requiring verification of the new address before the change takes effect.
- **FR-1.8** The system MUST let a signed-in user register additional passkeys, view their registered credentials, and revoke any of them (SEC-AUTHN-8 governs the revocation guard and notification; SEC-SESSION-5 the session consequences).
- **FR-1.9** Every create/read/update/delete/export operation MUST be permitted only for the authenticated owner of the data. SEC-AUTHZ-1 through SEC-AUTHZ-5 own how that is enforced and what a denied request may disclose.
- **FR-1.10** Sessions MUST expire after 7 days regardless of activity and MUST expire after 1 hour of inactivity.
- **FR-1.11** While an account has exactly one registered passkey, the system MUST prompt the signed-in user to register a second one (FR-1.8), so the loss of a single credential does not force account recovery (T-2). The prompt is a dismissible banner shown on the case list after each sign-in while the condition holds, and a dismissal lasts for that session only (D-34; `DESIGN.md` owns the prompt convention).
- **FR-1.12** The system MUST let the signed-in user view and change their timezone and locale (**Data Entities**) in account settings. The client MUST submit a detected IANA timezone and locale at registration to seed both, and the user MUST be able to change either at any time; SEC-INPUT-1 owns validation of the submitted values. Timezone governs the date-only boundaries NFR-6.1 fixes and locale the server-generated output NFR-5.4 fixes, so neither attribute is set once and left unreachable (D-23, D-24).

### 2. Accident Cases

- **FR-2.1** The system MUST allow a user to create a case with an accident date (required, MUST NOT be in the future) and optional time, location, description, and title; when no title is given the system MUST derive one from the accident date.
- **FR-2.2** The system MUST support multiple cases per user and MUST list them with open cases first, newest accident date first.
- **FR-2.3** The system MUST allow the user to edit any case field at any time except the currency, subject to the same validation as creation. The currency is fixed at creation and MUST NOT be editable afterwards (FR-7.5, D-18).
- **FR-2.4** The system MUST show a per-case overview containing at minimum: count of open tasks, count of overdue tasks (FR-5.5), next upcoming appointment, upcoming deadlines — the tasks flagged as hard deadlines (FR-5.7) whose due date falls within the next 7 days, not every dated task, which the overdue count already covers — expense totals (FR-7.3), and the 10 most recent timeline events. The seven-day window and the overdue flag evaluate in the user's timezone (NFR-6.1).
- **FR-2.5** The system MUST allow the user to archive a case (hiding it from the default list) and to reverse the archive at any time with no data loss. Archiving and unarchiving MUST each record a timeline event (FR-8.1), so a case leaving or rejoining the default list is never a silent state change (T-12). Archived cases MUST be reachable through an explicit filter on the case list (FR-2.2), not a separate screen.
- **FR-2.6** The system MUST allow the user to delete a case only after a fresh passkey re-authentication (as FR-9.3 requires for account deletion; `SECURITY.md` owns the step-up ceremony) and an explicit confirmation that states all contained records will be removed; deletion MUST remove all case data, and MUST be undoable for 30 days from the account-level trash (FR-3.8) before becoming permanent.

### 3. Documents & Paperwork

- **FR-3.1** The system MUST let the user upload files of types PDF, JPEG, PNG, HEIC, WebP, DOCX, XLSX, TXT, MP4, and MOV, up to 25 MB per file — except video (MP4, MOV), which may be up to 500 MB per file (dashcam and scene clips are in scope) — and MUST reject any other type or oversize file with an error naming the violated rule.
- **FR-3.2** Each document MUST have a title (defaulting to the filename) and exactly one category from: police & legal, insurance, medical record, bill/invoice, repair & vehicle, correspondence, receipt, identity, other; and MAY have a document date, notes, tags, and links to a contact, health issue, or expense (FR-6.3 governs what a link retains when its target is deleted).
- **FR-3.3** The system MUST list a case's documents with filtering by category, tag, and date range, and sorting by document date or upload date; when the client requests no sort, the list MUST default to upload date descending. Pagination and ordering determinism follow **10. List contract**.
- **FR-3.4** The system MUST provide text search over document titles, notes, and tags within a case. File contents are not indexed in v1 (no OCR); content search is revisited against NFR-4.3, and `ARCHITECTURE.md` owns the search technology.
- **FR-3.5** The system MUST let the user view PDFs and images in the app and download any document as the byte-identical original file.
- **FR-3.6** The system MUST let the user edit a document's metadata at any time without altering the stored file.
- **FR-3.7** The system MUST let the user replace a document's file while retaining access to prior versions. Prior versions are retained indefinitely and the system MUST NOT provide any operation that deletes one, so the authentic original always survives a doctored replacement (T-47); because they can therefore never be reclaimed, they do not count toward the FR-3.10 quota (D-21).
- **FR-3.8** Deleting a document MUST require confirmation (NFR-5.5); deleted documents MUST be recoverable for 30 days before permanent removal. Recovery MUST be offered through one trash surface per case, listing every soft-deleted record the case holds — documents, journal entries (FR-8.4), health issues and appointments (FR-4.11), tasks (FR-5.4), contacts (FR-6.3), and expenses (FR-7.2) — each showing the time remaining in its window and a restore action. Deleted cases (FR-2.6) are recovered from an account-level trash instead, since the case that would host the surface is itself gone. One trash per case rather than a trash per record type, so a user who deleted something in error has exactly one place to look while their 30 days run (D-36).
- **FR-3.9** The system MUST scan every upload for malware, and MUST quarantine a flagged file with a notice to the user. Scanning runs asynchronously in the background worker, so an upload is acknowledged before its scan completes (D-20): while a document's scan is pending it MUST appear in the case's document list marked as pending, and MUST NOT be downloadable, viewable in the app, or included in any export. A file the scan clears becomes fully available; a flagged file is quarantined and never becomes retrievable or exportable (SEC-FILE-4 owns quarantine handling; `ARCHITECTURE.md` owns the scanning engine and the worker isolation SEC-FILE-6 requires).
- **FR-3.10** The system MUST enforce a per-user storage quota of 25 GB, MUST reject uploads that would exceed it with an error showing current usage, and MUST display current usage on request. Current documents, trashed documents awaiting purge (FR-3.8), case-summary PDFs (FR-9.1), and expense CSVs (FR-7.6) count toward the quota. Retained prior versions (FR-3.7) and full-account export archives (FR-9.2) do NOT count toward it (D-19, D-21). The usage display MUST still break usage down across all of these, counted and exempt alike, so the user can see where space is held. Every counted category is reclaimable on a stated timer — trash purges after 30 days (FR-3.8) and the counted artifacts after 7 (FR-9.4) — so a session-holding attacker cannot silently exhaust the quota with space the user can never recover and block new evidence uploads before a deadline (T-21).

### 4. Health Issues & Appointments

- **FR-4.1** The system MUST let the user record a health issue with a name (required) and optional body area, onset date (MUST NOT be in the future), severity (integer 0–10), and description.
- **FR-4.2** A health issue MUST carry a status of active, improving, or resolved; the user MUST be able to change status at any time, and each change MUST be timestamped and visible in the issue's history.
- **FR-4.3** The system MUST let the user add timestamped progress updates (note plus optional severity 0–10) to a health issue and MUST display them in chronological order; severity over time SHOULD be shown as a chart.
- **FR-4.4** The system SHOULD let the user record treatments/medications on a health issue (name, start/end dates, prescribing provider, notes) as recordkeeping only.
- **FR-4.5** The system MUST let the user link providers (contacts), documents, expenses, and appointments to a health issue and navigate between linked records; FR-6.3 governs what a link retains when its target is deleted.
- **FR-4.6** The system MUST let the user create appointments with date/time (required; past or future), and optional provider (contact), location, purpose, and linked health issues; and MUST show an upcoming-appointments list per case.
- **FR-4.7** Creating a future appointment MUST create a default reminder 24 hours before it (via FR-5.6), which the user MAY change or remove.
- **FR-4.8** The system MUST let the user record outcome notes on an appointment after it occurs.
- **FR-4.9** The system MUST NOT present diagnoses, treatment recommendations, or any other medical advice; health features are recordkeeping only.
- **FR-4.10** The system MUST let the user edit a health issue, an appointment, a progress update, and a treatment at any time, subject to the same validation as creation (FR-4.1, FR-4.3, FR-4.4, FR-4.6); each edit MUST appear on the timeline (FR-8.1). This is the same edit capability tasks (FR-5.4), contacts (FR-6.3), and expenses (FR-7.2) already carry.
- **FR-4.11** The system MUST let the user delete a health issue, an appointment, a progress update, and a treatment after explicit confirmation (NFR-5.5). Deleting any of the four MUST be undoable for 30 days from the case trash (FR-3.8) before becoming permanent (D-42). Deleting a health issue MUST cascade to its progress updates and treatments, which are restored with it while its window remains open. Records that linked to a deleted health issue or appointment are governed by FR-6.3.

### 5. Recovery Tasks, Deadlines & Reminders

- **FR-5.1** On case creation the system MUST offer a starter recovery checklist that the user can accept in full, in part, or skip; it MUST include at least: notify insurance company; obtain the police/accident report; photograph vehicle damage; seek a medical evaluation; obtain repair estimates; arrange alternate transportation; notify employer and track missed work; review policy coverage and filing deadlines; start tracking expenses. This nine-item list is the final v1 content (D-12); the FR-5.8 guidance notice applies. Each accepted item MUST be created carrying its natural FR-5.2 category — insurance for notifying the insurer and reviewing policy coverage, legal for the police report, medical for the medical evaluation, vehicle for photographing damage and obtaining estimates, personal for transportation and notifying the employer, financial for expense tracking — so the checklist, which is most users' first encounter with tasks, models the categorisation the rest of FR-5 relies on (D-51).
- **FR-5.2** The system MUST let the user create a task with a title (required) and optional due date, notes, category (insurance, legal, medical, vehicle, financial, personal), and links to case records.
- **FR-5.3** The system MUST let the user mark a task complete or not complete; completion MUST be timestamped and MUST appear on the timeline (FR-8.1). Reverting a completion MUST also be timestamped and MUST appear on the timeline, so a completion never silently disappears from a case that tracks deadlines (D-52, T-12).
- **FR-5.4** The system MUST let the user edit and delete tasks; deletion MUST require confirmation (NFR-5.5) and MUST be undoable for 30 days from the case trash (FR-3.8) before becoming permanent (D-37).
- **FR-5.5** The system MUST visibly flag tasks whose due date has passed without completion, and include them in the case overview's overdue count (FR-2.4).
- **FR-5.6** The user MUST be able to set one or more reminder date-times on any task or appointment; the system MUST deliver an in-app notification within 5 minutes of each reminder time and SHOULD also deliver email. A reminder whose target record is in the case trash at fire time MUST be suppressed, and restoring the record MUST NOT fire it retroactively (D-54). A reminder whose fire time passed while the background worker was unavailable MUST be delivered late, marked as late, if its fire time is within the past hour, and MUST otherwise be skipped and logged (D-55) — so an outage cannot silently cost a filing deadline (T-49) without burying the user in stale alerts. the user MUST be able to turn email reminders on or off account-wide, and the toggle MUST default to on for a new account so the deadline safety net is present during the disoriented days right after an accident, before the user has visited settings (D-50).
- **FR-5.7** The user SHOULD be able to flag a dated task as a hard deadline, causing a visible countdown and escalating reminders at 30, 7, and 1 day(s) before the due date. Escalation points already passed when the task is flagged MUST be skipped rather than fired immediately, since a "30 days remaining" alert for a task due in ten would misstate the time left (D-57). Editing the due date MUST recompute the escalation set against the new date under the same skip rule; an escalation already delivered MUST NOT be re-sent (D-58).
- **FR-5.8** All guidance content (including the starter checklist) MUST be accompanied by a notice that it is general organizational guidance, not legal or medical advice.

### 6. Contacts

- **FR-6.1** The system MUST let the user add a contact to a case with a name (required), one role from the set in **Data Entities**, and optional phone, email, address, reference numbers (e.g., policy or claim numbers), and notes.
- **FR-6.2** Contacts are case-scoped: a contact MUST belong to exactly one case; a person involved in more than one case is entered in each (D-8).
- **FR-6.3** The system MUST let the user edit and delete contacts; deletion MUST require confirmation (NFR-5.5) and MUST be undoable for 30 days from the case trash (FR-3.8) before becoming permanent (D-37). Deleting any record that other records link to — a contact, a health issue or appointment (FR-4.11), a document (FR-3.8), or an expense (FR-7.2) — MUST NOT delete the journal entries, documents, or other records that referenced it: the link is severed and each referencing record MUST retain the deleted record's name or title as text. Restoring a record from the trash within its window MUST re-establish the links that severing removed, so an undo genuinely undoes; the retained text is the permanent fallback once the record is purged (D-43). This rule holds for every link type in **Data Entities** (D-27); `ARCHITECTURE.md` owns the resulting name/title snapshot in the persistence design. The deletion confirmation (NFR-5.5) MUST disclose that retention, because the retained text continues to appear in exports handed to third parties (T-42).
- **FR-6.4** The system MUST list a case's contacts filterable by role.

### 7. Expenses

- **FR-7.1** The system MUST let the user record an expense with amount (required, > 0, expressed to the case currency's ISO 4217 minor unit — FR-7.5) and date (required), one category from: medical, vehicle repair, rental/transport, lost wages, legal, insurance, other; a payment status of unpaid, paid, submitted for reimbursement, or reimbursed, which the user owns and the system MUST NOT change on their behalf (FR-7.3); and optional payee (contact), notes, linked receipt document, and linked health issue (FR-6.3 governs what a link retains when its target is deleted).
- **FR-7.2** The system MUST let the user edit expenses and delete them after confirmation (NFR-5.5); deletion MUST be undoable for 30 days from the case trash (FR-3.8) before becoming permanent (D-37).
- **FR-7.3** The system MUST show per-case expense totals: overall, by category, by payment status, and the total not yet reimbursed — the sum across the case of every expense's remaining balance (FR-7.4). Money is computed from reimbursement records and never from the payment status: recording a reimbursement MUST NOT change an expense's payment status, and no total other than the by-payment-status breakdown may be derived from that status (D-25).
- **FR-7.4** The system SHOULD support recording partial reimbursements (amount + date) against an expense and MUST, where supported, show the remaining balance, computed as the expense amount minus the sum of its recorded reimbursements. A reimbursement amount MUST be > 0, MUST be expressed to the case currency's ISO 4217 minor unit (FR-7.1), and MUST NOT exceed the expense's remaining balance at the time it is recorded.
- **FR-7.5** Each case records exactly one currency, chosen at case creation from the full set of ISO 4217 three-letter codes; all amounts and totals (FR-7.3) within a case use that currency, and the system MUST NOT perform exchange-rate conversion (D-15). That currency's ISO 4217 minor unit fixes the precision of every amount recorded in it (FR-7.1, D-28), so a zero-decimal currency such as JPY and a three-decimal currency such as KWD are each handled correctly. The currency MUST NOT change after creation (FR-2.3, D-18): because D-15 forbids conversion, a change would relabel already-recorded amounts without restating them, misstating evidence that FR-9.1 exports to insurers and attorneys.
- **FR-7.6** The system MUST export a case's expense list as CSV including all fields in FR-7.1 and the totals in FR-7.3.

### 8. Timeline & Journal

- **FR-8.1** The system MUST automatically record a timestamped timeline event when, at minimum: a case is created, edited, archived, or unarchived (FR-2.5); a document is added, has its file replaced (FR-3.7), or has its metadata edited (FR-3.6); a task is completed; an appointment is created, given outcome notes, or edited; an expense is added; a health issue is created, changes status, or is edited; a progress update or a treatment is created or edited (FR-4.10); and any record is deleted while its case survives (FR-4.11 for health records). Recording document replacement, metadata edits, health-record mutations, archiving, and deletions closes a silent-tampering gap: without them, an attacker with a stolen session could substitute a doctored evidence file, rewrite a severity history, archive a case out of sight, or delete records leaving no user-visible trace on the timeline (T-12, T-47). Records additionally carry independent tamper-evidence (content hashes, protected history) as specified in `SECURITY.md` (SEC-FILE-5).
- **FR-8.2** The system MUST let the user write journal entries (free text) with an entry date that MAY be in the past (backdating for events recalled later) but MUST NOT be in the future — evaluated in the user's timezone as NFR-6.1 requires — and optional links to a contact or other case records — e.g., a summary of a phone call with an adjuster. A journal is a contemporaneous record, and a future date would pin an entry above every real one in the FR-8.3 ordering (D-45).
- **FR-8.3** The system MUST display timeline events and journal entries together in date order (newest first by default) with filtering by type and date range.
- **FR-8.4** Journal entries MUST visibly show both their creation timestamp and last-edited timestamp; the system MUST retain and display prior versions of edited entries. A timeline event that records a deletion MUST survive the permanent purge of the record it refers to, as the journal-deletion event already does (D-17, D-46) — the content is gone, the fact of the deletion stays visible (T-12); every other event is removed with its source record. Users MUST NOT be able to edit or delete system-generated timeline events (they are removed only with their case or source record). A user MAY delete a journal entry after explicit confirmation (NFR-5.5); the deletion MUST be undoable for 30 days from the case trash (FR-3.8), and a permanent timeline event MUST record that an entry was deleted — the content is gone, the deletion stays visible (D-17, T-14).

### 9. Export & Data Management

- **FR-9.1** The system MUST generate a case summary as a PDF containing user-selected sections from: case details, contacts, document index, health issues with progress history, appointments, task status, expense report (FR-7.3), and the full timeline; generation MUST complete within 60 seconds, runs asynchronously, and MUST notify the user in-app when the file is ready to download, as FR-9.2 does for the full-account archive — so the budget is never an in-request wait the user watches (D-48).
- **FR-9.2** The system MUST let the user export all of their account data as a downloadable archive containing a machine-readable form (JSON or CSV) of every record plus every original uploaded file; the export runs asynchronously and MUST notify the user in-app when ready.
- **FR-9.3** The system MUST let the user delete their account after a fresh re-authentication and an explicit confirmation; all personal data MUST be permanently deleted within 30 days, and within 90 days from backups. The system MUST provide a 14-day cancellation window before purge begins — deletion initiated, notification email sent to the verified address (SEC-AUTHN-11), cancellable in-app for 14 days with a one-click cancel offered at sign-in, then purge — so that neither an adversary with the unlocked device nor the injured user in error can irreversibly destroy the entire evidentiary record with no notice and no way back (T-46). The window fits inside the 30/90-day deletion bounds above. Purge execution MUST delete the account at the managed identity provider (`ARCHITECTURE.md` names it) first, removing passkey credential material inside the 30-day bound, and MUST clear the first-party stores only afterwards, so a partially failed purge cannot leave usable credentials behind. A purge that misses the 30-day or the 90-day bound MUST raise a high-severity operator alert (D-38).
- **FR-9.4** Generated export artifacts stored in the file store — full-account archives (FR-9.2), case-summary PDFs (FR-9.1), and expense CSVs (FR-7.6) — MUST be purged by the background worker 7 days after generation; the user MAY regenerate any export at any time. Exports cover current records only: prior document versions (FR-3.7), prior journal versions (FR-8.4), and soft-deleted or trashed records are excluded, and each export MUST state this scope both to the user in the app and inside the produced artifact itself, so the insurer or attorney who receives it can see what it omits (D-49) — since a stale archive is a maximum-sensitivity aggregate that can silently retain records the user later deleted and can leak superseded drafts to attorneys and insurers if its scope is undeclared (T-18). Journal creation and last-edited timestamps (FR-8.4) ARE included in exports, and the system MUST show a one-time notice at journal-authoring time that these timestamps are permanent and appear in exports (T-42; `DESIGN.md` owns the notice convention). Case-summary PDFs (FR-9.1) and expense CSVs (FR-7.6) count toward the FR-3.10 quota; full-account archives (FR-9.2) do not, because such an archive necessarily contains every original file and so could never fit inside the quota it would be charged against, which would deny the FR-9.2 export — and with it the NFR-2.4 portability right — precisely to the users holding the most evidence (D-19).
- **FR-9.5** The system MUST purge accounts that have been inactive for 3 years, and MUST send warning emails to the verified address approximately 60 days and approximately 30 days before the purge (D-16). Inactivity MUST be measured from the User's last successful sign-in (**Data Entities**); nothing else resets the clock — not an authenticated request, a data write, or a fired reminder. FR-1.10's 7-day absolute session lifetime means a genuinely active user signs in again far inside the window (D-26).

### 10. List contract

- **FR-10.1** Every endpoint that returns a list MUST paginate it, with a server-set default page size and a documented maximum page size that a client request MUST NOT exceed; the pagination shape and the two values are recorded with the API surface in `ARCHITECTURE.md`. An unbounded list puts the NFR-4.1 latency budget at the mercy of how much evidence a case has accumulated.
- **FR-10.2** Every list ordering — including those FR-2.2, FR-3.3, FR-4.6, FR-6.4, and FR-8.3 specify — MUST end with a deterministic tiebreak on created timestamp, then record identifier, so paging across records that share a sort value never repeats or skips an entry (D-29).

## Non-Functional Requirements

Security (`NFR-1`) and privacy (`NFR-2`) requirements are authored in `SECURITY.md`, which owns them along with the controls that enforce them. Their IDs are unchanged and remain the reference mechanism.

### 3. Reliability

- **NFR-3.1** The service MUST target at least 99.5% monthly availability, excluding announced maintenance.
- **NFR-3.2** Automated backups MUST bound data loss to at most 24 hours (RPO ≤ 24 h) and restoration time to at most 12 hours (RTO ≤ 12 h); restores MUST be tested at least quarterly.
- **NFR-3.3** An upload acknowledged to the user as successful MUST remain durable and retrievable despite the failure of any single storage component.

### 4. Performance

- **NFR-4.1** Interactive reads and writes (excluding uploads, search, and exports) MUST complete in under 2 seconds at the 95th percentile under normal load of approximately 1,000 concurrent users (D-14).
- **NFR-4.2** A 25 MB upload MUST complete within 60 seconds on a 10 Mbps connection, and a larger upload MUST sustain no worse than that effective throughput — so a 500 MB video, the largest FR-3.1 permits, has a derived bound of roughly 20 minutes on the same connection. Stating the budget as a rate rather than a single fixed time gives every accepted file size a testable bound, including the slowest and most failure-prone path (D-53).
- **NFR-4.3** Document search (FR-3.4) MUST return results within 3 seconds at the 95th percentile.

### 5. Usability & Accessibility

- **NFR-5.1** All user-facing screens MUST conform to WCAG 2.2 Level AA. `DESIGN.md` **Accessibility** owns the design-language rules that achieve this.
- **NFR-5.2** All functionality MUST be usable on both mobile (viewport ≥ 360 px wide) and desktop form factors.
- **NFR-5.3** Error messages shown to users MUST state in plain language what happened and what the user can do next; raw technical errors MUST NOT be shown.
- **NFR-5.4** v1 ships in English and Spanish (D-15); user-facing copy SHOULD target roughly an 8th-grade reading level in every locale, since users may be injured, stressed, or medicated. Server-generated output produced with no client request in flight — reminder and security email, verification and recovery mail, FR-9.5 inactivity warnings, and FR-9.1 case-summary PDFs — MUST render in the User's locale (**Data Entities**), falling back to English when it is unset (D-24).
- **NFR-5.5** Every destructive action (delete case, document, health issue, appointment, progress update, treatment, task, contact, expense, journal entry, or account) MUST require explicit confirmation before execution. The confirmation copy MUST truthfully state the recovery timeline: the 30-day recovery window where one applies (FR-2.6, FR-3.8, FR-4.11, FR-5.4, FR-6.3, FR-7.2, FR-8.4), that deletion becomes permanent afterward, and that purged records persist in backups only for the bounded period SEC-DATA-8 sets (resolves CQ-3's copy conflict, T-33).

### 6. Data Integrity

- **NFR-6.1** Every record MUST carry created and last-updated timestamps, stored in UTC and displayed in the user's timezone (**Data Entities**). Date-only boundaries MUST be evaluated in the user's timezone rather than UTC: the not-in-the-future rules (FR-2.1, FR-4.1), the overdue flag (FR-5.5), and the FR-2.4 seven-day deadline window all resolve against the user's local day (D-23).
- **NFR-6.2** Monetary values MUST be stored and computed exactly (no floating-point rounding errors); all totals MUST be accurate to the case currency's ISO 4217 minor unit (FR-7.1, D-28) — which is the cent for a two-decimal currency, the whole unit for a zero-decimal currency such as JPY, and the thousandth for a three-decimal currency such as KWD (D-40).

## Decisions

Decisions that requirements elaborate. Any may be revisited; requirements cite them for traceability.

- **D-1** "Paperwork" = user-uploaded files with metadata, categories, search, and export (FR-3), plus date-driven obligations handled as tasks/deadlines (FR-5). Structured claim-form authoring is out of scope v1.
- **D-2** v1 is strictly single-user: one actor (the accident victim), one role, authenticated per FR-1, hard per-user isolation per FR-1.9. Confirmed for v1 (D-11); sharing/helper access is revisited after v1.
- **D-3** Health issue model per FR-4/**Data Entities**: named issue with severity 0–10, status lifecycle active→improving→resolved, timestamped progress updates, optional treatments, linked providers/documents/expenses/appointments.
- **D-4** "Get your life back on track" = guided starter checklist + tasks/deadlines/reminders (FR-5) + expense tracking (FR-7) + journal/timeline (FR-8) + shareable exports (FR-9).
- **D-5** All user data is treated as sensitive personal data (it includes health information) and protected per `SECURITY.md` under the GDPR-grade global bar (D-10); health-specific regime applicability is recorded in `SECURITY.md` (SQ-1 resolution).
- **D-6** A user can manage multiple accidents; the Case is the anchor entity (FR-2).
- **D-7** v1 has no integrations with insurers, healthcare systems, or any other third-party data source; all data is manually entered, and exports (FR-9) serve insurers/attorneys/providers instead. The external service dependencies are outbound email delivery (FR-1.1, FR-1.6, FR-1.7, FR-5.6) and the managed identity service behind authentication and recovery (FR-1.2, FR-1.6); `ARCHITECTURE.md` owns the products. Platform infrastructure (hosting, CDN) is not an integration.
- **D-8** Values originally chosen as provisional defaults to make requirements testable are final, each stated in its owning requirement: accepted file types and per-file size limits (FR-3.1), storage quota (FR-3.10), per-case currency (FR-7.5), v1 locales (NFR-5.4), case-scoped contacts (FR-6.2), session lifetimes (FR-1.10), starter checklist content (FR-5.1).
- **D-9** Passkeys (FR-1.2) are the only credential that grants normal account access. The managed identity service's required additional first factor is email OTP; passwords and SMS OTP remain disabled. The API converts a successful email-code ceremony only into a restricted first-passkey or lost-passkey recovery session that cannot read or modify account or case data. The one-time email code expires after 10 minutes, and recovery concludes with replacement-passkey registration under SEC-AUTHN-6. This accepts the verified mailbox as the recovery trust anchor while preserving passkey proof for every normal and high-impact operation; `ARCHITECTURE.md` owns the provider and its exact factor configuration.
- **D-10** v1 accepts users anywhere and designs to a GDPR-grade bar globally — lawful basis, data-subject rights, 72-hour breach notice, storage limitation — so jurisdiction never needs re-litigating; `SECURITY.md` owns the controls that implement the bar (resolves OQ-1).
- **D-11** v1 stays strictly single-user (D-2 confirmed): no sharing of any kind; exports (FR-9) are the sharing mechanism; helper/attorney access is revisited after v1 (resolves OQ-2).
- **D-12** The nine-item starter checklist in FR-5.1 is the final v1 content, presented as general guidance only (FR-4.9, FR-5.8); domain review by insurance/legal expertise is a post-v1 improvement, not a launch blocker (resolves OQ-3).
- **D-13** Notification channels are in-app and email only; SMS and push stay out of scope for v1, and no quiet-hours feature ships. The in-app feed serves as the notice of email delivery failure (SEC-EXT-4) (resolves OQ-4).
- **D-14** v1 is sized for a marketed public launch at approximately 1,000 concurrent users; NFR-4 budgets and the hosting sizing in `ARCHITECTURE.md` cite this figure (resolves OQ-5).
- **D-15** v1 ships localized: i18n infrastructure with English and Spanish locales (NFR-5.4), locale-aware date and number formatting, and one currency per case chosen at creation with no exchange-rate math (FR-7.5) (resolves OQ-6).
- **D-16** Accounts inactive for 3 years are purged after advance email warnings (FR-9.5); this satisfies the storage-limitation half of D-10's bar and anchors the export-artifact lifetime (FR-9.4) (resolves OQ-7).
- **D-17** Journal entries are deletable: explicit confirmation (NFR-5.5), a 30-day undo window, and a permanent timeline event recording the deletion — content gone, deletion visible (FR-8.4). System-generated timeline events remain undeletable (resolves OQ-8).
- **D-18** Case currency is immutable after creation. FR-2.3's "edit any case field" carves out currency, and FR-7.5 states the prohibition directly. This resolves a direct contradiction between the two: because D-15 forbids exchange-rate conversion, permitting a currency change would relabel already-recorded amounts without restating them, misstating the expense evidence FR-9.1 and FR-7.6 export to insurers and attorneys.
- **D-19** Full-account export archives (FR-9.2) do not count toward the FR-3.10 storage quota; case-summary PDFs (FR-9.1) and expense CSVs (FR-7.6) still do. This resolves an arithmetic contradiction: an archive contains every original file by definition, so charging it against the same 25 GB quota made the export impossible above roughly half the quota and denied the NFR-2.4/SEC-DATA-4 portability right exactly to the users holding the most evidence. The FR-9.4 seven-day artifact purge continues to bound archive lifetime, so the T-18 stale-aggregate concern is unaffected.
- **D-20** Malware scanning is asynchronous, and a document whose scan is pending is listed but not downloadable, viewable, or exportable (FR-3.9). This resolves the contradiction between FR-3.9's synchronous wording and `ARCHITECTURE.md`'s placement of scanning in the background worker: the worker isolation SEC-FILE-6 requires makes pre-acknowledgement scanning impossible, so the requirement states the interim state rather than implying one that cannot exist. The pending state closes the T-16 redistribution window, since a file cannot be retrieved or exported before it is cleared.
- **D-21** Retained prior document versions (FR-3.7) do not count toward the FR-3.10 quota, and no operation deletes one. This resolves the contradiction between FR-3.10's "so space is reclaimable" purpose and the absence of any reclaim path for prior versions. Exempting them is preferred over adding a deletion operation because FR-3.7's retention was raised to MUST precisely so the authentic original survives a doctored replacement (T-47); a user-facing delete would hand that capability to anyone holding a session.
- **D-22** Health issues, appointments, progress updates, and treatments are fully editable and deletable (FR-4.10, FR-4.11); health-issue and appointment deletion carries the same 30-day undo window as FR-2.6 and FR-3.8, and deleting a health issue cascades to its progress updates and treatments. Section 4 previously granted creation plus only the FR-4.2 status change and FR-4.8 outcome notes, while FR-5.4, FR-6.3, and FR-7.2 granted edit and delete for their entities and FR-1.9, FR-8.1, and SEC-LOG-1 all presupposed a general delete capability: one deletion semantic across every record type is what the rest of the document already assumed (resolves OQ-9).
- **D-23** The client detects the user's IANA timezone at registration and the user changes it in settings (FR-1.12); date-only boundaries evaluate in that timezone while storage stays UTC (NFR-6.1). Nothing previously created or updated the attribute NFR-6.1 depended on, and no rule fixed which timezone evaluated "MUST NOT be in the future" (FR-2.1, FR-4.1), "due date has passed" (FR-5.5), or FR-2.4's seven-day window — so conforming implementations could differ by a full day on validation and overdue flagging (resolves OQ-10).
- **D-24** The User carries a locale, seeded from the client at registration and changeable in settings (FR-1.12); all server-generated output renders in it and falls back to English when it is unset (NFR-5.4). Output produced with no client request in flight — reminder and security mail, verification and recovery mail, FR-9.5 warnings, FR-9.1 PDFs — otherwise had no defined locale at all, even though D-15 commits v1 to English and Spanish (resolves OQ-11).
- **D-25** Money is computed from reimbursement records and payment status is a user-owned label: an expense's remaining balance is its amount minus the sum of its reimbursements (FR-7.4), FR-7.3's total not yet reimbursed is the sum of those balances, and recording a reimbursement never changes the status. The two readings of "not yet reimbursed" — status not `reimbursed`, versus amount minus recorded reimbursements — disagree by real money the moment a partial reimbursement exists, and these figures are evidence FR-9.1 and FR-7.6 export to insurers and attorneys (resolves OQ-12).
- **D-26** Account inactivity (FR-9.5) is measured from the last successful sign-in, recorded on User (**Data Entities**), and nothing else resets the clock. FR-9.5 named no clock at all, unlike every other purge here; FR-1.10's 7-day absolute session lifetime means a genuinely active user signs in again far inside the 3-year window, so the narrow definition costs no real user their records (resolves OQ-13).
- **D-27** Deleting a linked record severs the link and leaves each referencing record holding the deleted record's name or title as plain text (FR-6.3). FR-6.3's contact-specific rule was the only stated behavior, leaving deletion of a receipt document, a linked health issue, or a linked expense undefined; generalizing the rule already in the document keeps one behavior across every link type, and the NFR-5.5 confirmation discloses the retention because the retained text travels into exports handed to third parties (T-42) (resolves OQ-14).
- **D-28** Case currency is an ISO 4217 three-letter code drawn from the full set, and that currency's ISO 4217 minor unit — not a fixed two decimal places — fixes the precision of every amount recorded in it (FR-7.1, FR-7.5). FR-7.1's two-decimal rule silently mis-handled zero-decimal currencies such as JPY and three-decimal currencies such as KWD; NFR-6.2's exact-arithmetic rule is unchanged.
- **D-29** Every list endpoint paginates with a default and a maximum page size, and every ordering ends with a deterministic tiebreak on created timestamp then record identifier (FR-10.1, FR-10.2); documents default to upload date descending when the client requests no sort (FR-3.3). An unbounded list makes NFR-4.1's latency budget a function of how much evidence a case has accumulated, and an ordering with ties lets paging repeat or skip records — in a document set that is evidence, a silently skipped row is a missing exhibit. `ARCHITECTURE.md` owns the API shape and the page-size values.
- **D-30** Devices are modeled as an entity (**Data Entities**) — opaque identifier, first-seen and last-seen timestamps — capped at 10 per account with least-recently-seen eviction. SEC-AUTHN-11's unrecognized-sign-in notice needs a stored device record to compare against, and 10 covers a phone, a tablet, a laptop, a work machine, and several browser profiles, so eviction is rare and the re-notification it eventually causes is worth the user's attention. The entity purges with the account (FR-9.3) and with the inactivity purge (FR-9.5), exports with it (FR-9.2), and is disclosed in the privacy policy (SEC-DATA-7).
- **D-31** "Flagged sensitive" is an entity-level classification recorded with **Data Entities**: health issues, progress updates, treatments, appointments, journal entries, and document titles and notes are flagged; case, task, contact, and expense metadata is not. `DESIGN.md`'s masking convention and SEC-SESSION-7's keyboard-flag rule need one predicate between them, and a field-by-field rule would leave the user unable to predict whether a screen is safe to open in a waiting room.
- **D-32** Free-text field lengths are fixed per class in **Data Entities** and enforced by SEC-INPUT-1. Requirements referred to titles, notes, tags, and journal text with no bound, leaving validation, storage sizing, and the NFR-5.3 rejection message undefined; the classes are set generously enough that a legitimate long journal entry is never truncated. The fields this decision left outside its five classes were bounded shortly afterwards by D-41, so every free-text field now carries a limit.
- **D-33** FR-2.4's overview shows the 10 most recent timeline events, and its "upcoming deadlines" are the FR-5.7 hard-deadline tasks falling due within 7 days rather than every dated task. The overdue count (FR-5.5) already covers dated tasks generally, so counting them again would make the overview noisier the more diligently a user records them.
- **D-34** The FR-1.11 second-passkey prompt is a dismissible banner on the case list after each sign-in while exactly one passkey is registered, and a dismissal lasts for that session only. It keeps returning until the user acts, without becoming a modal that blocks someone who opened the app to check a deadline; `DESIGN.md` owns the prompt convention.
- **D-35** Archiving and unarchiving a case each emit an FR-8.1 timeline event, and archived cases are reached through an explicit filter on the existing case list rather than a separate screen (FR-2.5). A case leaving the default list is a state change an attacker holding a session could use to hide evidence from its owner, so it is never silent (T-12); a filter also leaves one list to learn instead of two.
- **D-36** One trash surface per case lists every soft-deleted record it holds, each with its remaining window and a restore action, and deleted cases are recovered from an account-level trash (FR-3.8). FR-3.8's document trash generalizes rather than spawning a trash per record type, so a user who deleted something in error has exactly one place to look while their 30 days run.
- **D-37** Tasks, contacts, and expenses gain the same 30-day undo window as every other deletable record (FR-5.4, FR-6.3, FR-7.2). One deletion semantic across every record type is what completes the FR-3.8 trash, and an expense is evidence of real money that should survive a mis-tap; NFR-5.5's confirmation copy states the window for all three.
- **D-38** FR-9.3 purge execution deletes the account at the managed identity provider first and clears first-party stores afterwards, and a purge that misses the 30-day or the 90-day bound raises a high-severity operator alert. Removing credential material first means a partially failed purge cannot leave a usable passkey behind, and those bounds are the erasure promise SEC-DATA-4 rests on, so they must not fail silently.
- **D-39** One pre-launch legal review is commissioned, covering health-content and unlicensed-practice exposure, the sufficiency of the FR-4.9 and FR-5.8 notices, and accessibility obligations in the launch markets; its outcomes are recorded in the documents that own them, and it is gated by the SEC-DEPLOY-4 release checklist. Until it reports, the **Jurisdiction / Regulatory Scope** and **Regulatory** fields of requirements authored from `REQUIREMENT_TEMPLATE.md` stay `TO BE DECIDED` — deliberately an honest unassessed state rather than a guessed mapping. D-10's GDPR-grade global bar and the SQ-1 outcome in `SECURITY.md` remain the standing posture meanwhile.
- **D-40** NFR-6.2's exactness is expressed in the case currency's ISO 4217 minor unit rather than "the cent". Once D-28 made precision currency-dependent, "accurate to the cent" was literally false for zero- and three-decimal currencies; the arithmetic rule is unchanged and nothing changes for a two-decimal currency.
- **D-41** The short free-text fields no length class covered — Case and Appointment location, Appointment purpose, Health Issue body area, Contact address — are bounded at 500 characters, and Contact phone and email at 200. D-32 fixed five classes and left these unbounded, which SEC-INPUT-1 cannot enforce; 500 is generous for a full postal address or a described body area without leaving the field effectively unbounded.
- **D-42** Deleting a progress update or a treatment carries the same 30-day undo as a health issue or appointment (FR-4.11). D-22 granted the window only to the parent records, which would have left two deletion semantics inside one health record — an issue recoverable, its progress notes not — and made the NFR-5.5 confirmation copy differ between them for no stated reason.
- **D-43** Restoring a record from the trash re-establishes the links severing removed (FR-6.3). D-27 severed links at deletion and D-37 then made the targets recoverable, so without this an undo would return the record while silently discarding relationships the user never chose to break — the receipt would come back but no longer sit against its expense.
- **D-44** Masked sensitive content is revealed one element at a time and re-masks on navigation (`DESIGN.md` **On-Screen Privacy**). DQ-6's threat is an observer glancing at a shared or observed device, so a reveal that persisted across screens or for a session would leave the record exposed long after the user's deliberate act.
- **D-45** A journal entry date MUST NOT be in the future (FR-8.2). FR-8.2 permitted backdating and said nothing about forward dating, and SEC-INPUT-1's future-date prohibitions named only accident and onset dates; a mistyped year would have pinned an entry above every real one in the FR-8.3 ordering permanently.
- **D-46** A timeline event recording a deletion survives the permanent purge of the record it refers to; every other event is removed with its source (FR-8.4). D-17 already ruled this for journal entries, but FR-8.4's general "removed only with their case or source record" left the rest ambiguous — and an attacker who deleted evidence and waited out the undo window would otherwise leave no trace that anything had existed (T-12).
- **D-47** The Notification `type` attribute is a closed set of four values, and read state changes only on an explicit act. The specs named four kinds of feed content without enumerating them, so no client could handle the feed exhaustively; and with no read-state rule, a feed that marked everything read on open would silently consume the security messages SEC-AUTHN-11 relies on being noticed (T-27).
- **D-48** The FR-9.1 case-summary PDF is generated asynchronously and its readiness is announced by an in-app notification, as FR-9.2's archive already is. FR-9.1 stated only "downloadable in-app" plus a 60-second budget, which would have made a minute-long in-request wait the normal case — worst on the mobile connections this product is most used on.
- **D-49** Each export states its scope inside the produced artifact as well as in the app (FR-9.4). T-18's harm falls on the recipient — an insurer or attorney — who cannot see the in-app statement; only an embedded one travels with the file.
- **D-50** The FR-5.6 account-wide email-reminder toggle defaults to on. The product exists to stop an injured user missing insurance and legal deadlines, and FR-5.7's escalating hard-deadline reminders are worth little if the channel is silent until the user finds a settings screen.
- **D-51** Starter-checklist tasks are created carrying their natural FR-5.2 category (FR-5.1). The checklist is most users' first encounter with tasks, so creating nine uncategorised items would have modelled not using the categorisation the rest of FR-5 depends on.
- **D-52** Reverting a task completion emits a timeline event, as completing it does (FR-5.3, FR-8.1). FR-8.1's list is a minimum and omitted the reverse transition, which would have let a recorded completion silently disappear from a case whose whole purpose is tracking deadlines (T-12).
- **D-53** NFR-4.2's upload budget is expressed as a throughput floor rather than a single fixed time, so every accepted file size has a bound. FR-3.1 permits 500 MB video while NFR-4.2 named only 25 MB, leaving the slowest and most failure-prone path — dashcam footage over a mobile connection — governed by no performance requirement at all.
- **D-54** A reminder whose target sits in the case trash is suppressed and is not fired retroactively on restore (FR-5.6). D-37's undo windows made trashed targets a routine case; notifying a user about a record they deleted would confuse, and a restore should not produce a burst of stale alerts.
- **D-55** A reminder missed while the worker was unavailable is delivered late and marked late within a one-hour grace window, and skipped with a log entry beyond it (FR-5.6). Silently dropping it would let an outage cost a filing deadline (T-49); delivering every stale reminder would bury the user in alerts for events that already happened.
- **D-56** Clients learn of new feed items by polling on an interval while foregrounded plus refreshing on navigation, at an interval inside FR-5.6's five-minute budget (`ARCHITECTURE.md`). Push would meet the budget with room to spare but adds a long-lived connection through the edge for notifications that are rarely urgent to the second, and refresh-only would miss the budget outright for a user sitting on one screen.
- **D-57** Escalation points already passed when a task is flagged as a hard deadline are skipped, not fired immediately (FR-5.7).
- **D-58** Editing a hard deadline's due date recomputes its escalation set against the new date, without re-sending an escalation already delivered (FR-5.7). A deadline that moves must carry its warnings with it, or the FR-5.7 countdown silently refers to a date that no longer exists.
